About

Geospatial data scientist working with large, multi-source forest and environmental datasets. First author of a forthcoming FEMC regional report comparing regeneration and overstory composition across more than 10,000 monitoring plots in the Northeastern U.S., built on R pipelines I wrote to standardize and analyze the underlying inventory data.

I also build automated Python geoprocessing workflows, and since 2023 have worked remotely and largely independently, turning inventory and remote-sensing records into analysis-ready results for foresters, land managers, and researchers.

Selected Projects

Northeastern Forest Regeneration

FEMC · Forthcoming 2026 · First author

A regional analysis comparing overstory and regeneration composition across 10,632 forest monitoring plots (2015–2024), using compositional methods (Bray-Curtis dissimilarity, Shannon diversity) to flag the forest types where sapling and seedling layers diverge most from the current canopy. Built on R pipelines that harmonize FIA, FHM, and NEFIN inventory data into a single analysis-ready dataset.

Distance-to-Infrastructure Rasters

FEMC · 2024–present

An automated Python pipeline generating 30 annual 10 m distance-to-infrastructure rasters (roads, buildings, and combined infrastructure, 2016–2025) for the Northeastern U.S. as inputs to a forest-disturbance model. Retrieves TIGER/Line and OpenStreetMap (ohsome) data automatically and validates against Microsoft Building Footprints.

High-Resolution Land Cover & Tree Canopy

UVM Spatial Analysis Lab · 2023–2025

Classification and multi-pass quality review of high-resolution land cover — tree canopy, impervious surface, buildings, and road networks — across 18 mapping projects in 15+ US states and Canada (~2,760 hours in ArcGIS Pro). Programs included the Chesapeake Bay Watershed, EPA Area 3, Texas statewide, the California Central Coast, and New York City, with LiDAR point-cloud processing in QTM.

Dissolved Oxygen & Phosphorus Cycling

UVM Honors Thesis · 2023

Undergraduate honors thesis on dissolved-oxygen dynamics and their implications for phosphorus cycling in restored wetlands of the Lake Champlain Basin, combining lab methods (TSS/TVS, loss-on-ignition) with field-deployed YSI multiparameter sensors, analyzed and visualized in R.
